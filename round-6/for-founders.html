1:1 Founder Coaching for Business Owners

As a founder or business owner, you may know the decision in front of you and still keep putting it off. Founder coaching with Victor Pierantoni focuses on your part in that situation: the assumptions you are making, the feelings or expectations that hold your attention and the action you want to consider. A difficult conversation, a question of trust or the pressure to keep achieving can give the work a specific starting point.

This individual offer is for adults aged 18 and older.

Founder Coaching for a Decision You Keep Reopening

What is settled, and what are you still trying to find out? You might bring a choice you keep reconsidering, along with the information you have and the question that remains unanswered.

As a business grows, a founder or CEO may face a change in their own responsibilities: deciding what to keep doing, what to hand over and how to explain those choices to the people they lead. For example, a startup founder who once handled every customer request may now keep taking work back from someone they hired to own it. Describe what changed, what you find difficult to let go of and what you still need to decide. This is an illustration of a changing role; the company structure or commercial decision may also need experienced business advice.

A Conversation You Keep Postponing

You may know what needs discussing but feel caught up in how the other person could respond. Bring what has happened, what you want to say and what makes the conversation difficult for you.

The Pressure to Keep Achieving

Work can take up so much attention that time away and other interests receive very little of it. You may want to explore what achievement means to you, what you have put aside and what you want more room for in your life.

Example: Preparing a Conversation About Responsibilities

Imagine a founder who keeps postponing a conversation about who owns a recurring task. Each delay adds frustration, but the founder has not yet asked how the other person understands the responsibility.

A useful starting point is to separate the event from the interpretation: the task was missed; the founder expects a defensive reply; the other person's account is still unknown. The next question could be, “How do you understand who is responsible for this?” That gives the founder something specific to ask before deciding what the missed task means or what should change.

This is a fictional illustration of a question you could bring to coaching. It is not a client result or a recommendation for handling every business disagreement.

What Would Make the Conversation Useful?

If several issues compete for attention, list them and choose one to begin with. Which is holding up a decision you need to make, a conversation you keep avoiding or work that matters to you? State what makes that issue the priority.

Honest reflection can include noticing your own part in the situation and saying when an interpretation does not fit what happened. Consider whether you want that kind of conversation, and ask how questions or disagreement are handled before you commit.

Name something you would like to leave with: a clearer question, the opening of a difficult conversation or a next action you are willing to take. Afterwards, look at what you actually did and learned. Did you ask the question? Did new information change your view? What still needs attention?

These are ways to make your own intention concrete. They do not promise a particular decision, revenue increase or response from someone else. Read what a coaching session can involve for a fuller picture of the approach.

Match the Help to the Question

A founder coach can help examine your hesitation, expectations and response to a situation. A business consultant or another qualified adviser may be needed to assess a commercial, legal, financial or technical question. Choose the support that addresses the actual question; you remain responsible for your company's decisions and the advice you rely on.

In the reflective approach described here, a practical decision is examined alongside the feelings and assumptions surrounding it.
