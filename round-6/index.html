For Founders and Business Owners

Leading a business can put you between a decision and the people who need an answer. You might be postponing feedback to a colleague or deciding which responsibilities to hand over. Start with a situation in your business and the response you want to change.

What is settled, and what are you still trying to find out? You might bring a choice you keep reconsidering, along with the information you have and the question that remains unanswered.

You may know what needs discussing but feel caught up in how the other person could respond. Bring what has happened, what you want to say and what makes the conversation difficult for you.

Work can take up so much attention that time away and other interests receive very little of it. You may want to explore what achievement means to you, what you have put aside and what you want more room for in your life.

For Adult Athletes

You may trust a skill in practice and begin to doubt it in competition. Pressure can pull your concentration toward the last mistake or what might happen next. Start with a moment when your focus or confidence changed and what you noticed.

Describe what happened and where your attention went next. You may want to understand the replay in your head, the meaning you gave the mistake or what you wanted to do in the next moment.

Bring one example of the difference. What did you notice before, during and after it? You do not need to diagnose the cause to explain the question you want help with.

New expectations, attention and requests from other people can raise questions beyond the next performance. You may want to explore a conversation you need to have, a request you are unsure about or a boundary that matters to you.

What You Can Work On With Victor

You might call it a mental block: you keep second-guessing a decision or putting off a conversation. Start with that specific situation.

  1. Victor's reflective questions distinguish an event from the interpretation you give it.

  2. The discussion can examine a belief or expectation, consider another view and identify an action connected to the situation.

  3. Illustration: suppose you are postponing a difficult conversation because you expect it to go badly. A coaching question could examine what you know about the situation, what you are predicting and what you need to clarify before deciding what to say.

These are examples from his coaching and teaching, not a fixed script for every session.

In performance coaching, you and Victor work together on those questions. Personal growth can mean a change you choose in your own life, such as asking a question you have avoided, checking an assumption or taking an action you chose. You can then review what you did and what you learned.
