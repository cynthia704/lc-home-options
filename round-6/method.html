What Happens in a Coaching Session With Victor?

What happens in a coaching session depends on the situation you bring and what you want from the conversation. Victor's questions can examine the events, your interpretation and a next action. His teaching also includes attention to feelings and bodily sensations.

The examples below explain parts of Victor's coaching process. They are possible activities, rather than a sequence every session follows.

Start With the Part You Can Describe

You might know that a conversation is unresolved without knowing what to say. Or you may keep returning to a decision without knowing which question would help. Describe what happened and what still has your attention.

Finding a more specific question can itself be a useful focus for the conversation.

For an initial inquiry, a few sentences about the situation and what you want to discuss are enough. Leave out other people's names and sensitive details that are not needed to explain the question. Check how information will be handled before sharing anything more.

What Happened, and What Are You Making It Mean?

Victor's reflective questions distinguish an event from the interpretation you give it. The discussion can examine a belief or expectation, consider another view and identify an action connected to the situation.

Illustration: suppose you are postponing a difficult conversation because you expect it to go badly. A coaching question could examine what you know about the situation, what you are predicting and what you need to clarify before deciding what to say.

How Do Feelings Enter the Work?

Victor's emotional-awareness teaching directs attention toward bodily sensations associated with a feeling. Reflective questioning examines events and interpretations; noticing sensations attends to the experience of the feeling itself.

An attention or breathing exercise is another activity shown in Victor's work. These descriptions help explain his teaching without asking you to perform an exercise from this page.

Emotional Debt

Emotional Debt is Victor's metaphor for emotions he believes have not been fully felt or processed and still influence a person's responses. The word debt points to something he considers unaddressed. This is an account of his coaching idea, not a diagnosis, a measure of health or proof of what causes a particular difficulty.

An unfinished conversation or a situation you keep avoiding can give you something specific to discuss. Begin with what happened and how you responded, rather than assuming the metaphor explains it.

Choose How You Want to Take Action

The action you consider might be a question to ask, a conversation to prepare or an assumption to check. These are possible ways to use a conversation in daily life; they do not promise a particular business or sporting result.
